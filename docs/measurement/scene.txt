The rain had not stopped since Tuesday, and the lane outside was a river of brown water.
Meera set the empty glass on the sill, where the light would catch it.
Arjun came in shaking rain from his blue coat, and the doorway filled with the smell of wet earth.
"You left the window open again," he said, hanging the coat on the brass hook.
She did not answer. The glass stood where she had left it, untouched.
Outside, a full moon was trying to rise behind the clouds.
He poured the tea into two steel cups and pushed one toward her.
"The roof is leaking over the cot again," Meera said.
Arjun dragged the bucket under the drip, his coat still dripping on the tiles.
For a while they only listened to the rain hammering the tin.
Then Meera reached for the jug, filled the glass to the brim, and drank.
Arjun stared. "You never drink before the lamps are lit."
"It is a new house," she said, "and new houses have new rules."
The glass caught the lamplight when she set it down again.
From the lane came the sound of a bicycle bell, twice, then nothing.
Arjun took his coat from the hook and shook the rain out of its sleeves.
The bucket was filling faster now; water brimmed over its edge onto the floor.
"You will catch a fever," he said, "standing in wet clothes."
Meera laughed for the first time that week.
She wiped the glass with the edge of her sari and put it back on the sill.
Beyond the window the rain had thinned to a silver thread.
Arjun buttoned the coat against the draught from the door.
"We leave at dawn," he said, "if the lane is passable."
Meera nodded, and blew out the lamp.
